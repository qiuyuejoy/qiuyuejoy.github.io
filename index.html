Joy Qiuyue Zhong

Computer Science Ph.D. Student in HCI @ UMass Amherst

Hello, and welcome to my work and world! I’m Joy Qiuyue Zhong, a Computer Science Ph.D. student in Human-Computer Interaction at the University of Massachusetts Amherst, advised by Professor Ravi Karkar.

It’s so exciting and freeing to explore research questions through both humanistic and technical lenses! I hold an M.S. in Information Systems from Northeastern University and a Master of Laws (LL.M.) from Washington University in St. Louis. Together with my earlier experience in business and entrepreneurship, this training shapes how I think about people’s needs, organizational practices, and the legal and ethical dimensions of technology.

This generalist mindset guides my research on how technology can support individual and collective well-being. Across related projects, I draw on psychology, sociology, and philosophy to understand people’s needs, values, and experiences, and examine how technology can support meaningful social interaction and human–AI collaboration. These interests extend to societal AI, where I seek to translate legal and ethical insights into frameworks for AI design, evaluation, and governance.

My goal is to help shape technology that responds to individual needs and respects human autonomy while accounting for diverse social values and the principles and purposes underlying law.
